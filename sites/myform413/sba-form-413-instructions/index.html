SBA Form 413 Instructions: All 147 Fields, Field by Field

The official SBA Form 413 Personal Financial Statement (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188, expires 08/31/2027) is a six-page PDF with 147 fillable fields, all of them on pages 1 to 4. This page lists every one in the order the form prints it, with what to enter and the common mistake to avoid.

Fill the form with the guided tool →

Every instruction below comes from the wording printed on the official form. Quoted text is the form's own. This page explains how to complete the form; it does not predict any decision on your application and is not legal, tax or accounting advice.

  10. Authorization, certification and signatures (page 4) (8 fields)

A "×5" beside a field name means the same box repeats that many times, once per row or column. Counted that way the ten groups add up to 147.

Program checkboxes (page 1) 4 fields

Page 1 opens with the form's own three steps: "Check all that apply," "Complete the form in its entirety," and "Review the applicable certifications and sign (spousal signature, if required)." The four checkboxes are step one.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
7(a) loan / 504 loan / Surety BondsCheck it if the statement supports a 7(a) loan, a 504 loan or a surety bond guarantee. The form asks for one statement from each proprietor, general partner, managing member of an LLC, each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant, and any person providing a guaranty on the loan.Leaving every box unchecked. The box you check decides which "who must complete" rule and which freshness window apply to you.
Disaster Business Loan ApplicationCheck it for a disaster business loan. The form asks for one statement from each applicant, general partner, managing member of an LLC, owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant business, and any person providing an unlimited guaranty.Using the 120-day window. For Disaster the form prints 90 days (see "This information is current as of").
Women Owned Small Business (WOSB) Federal Contracting ProgramCheck it if you are claiming economic disadvantage under the WOSB program. The form requires a separate form from your spouse unless you are legally separated, and asks you to update the form as changes arise, at least annually.Skipping the "WOSB applicant only, Married" Yes/No boxes on page 2, which exist only for this program.
8(a) Business Development ProgramCheck it if you are a socially and economically disadvantaged individual applying for 8(a) certification. If married, the form requires a separate Form 413 from your spouse unless you are legally separated (then provide a copy of the separation document).Entering business figures. The 8(a) block says to complete the form "with Personal Information not Business Information" and to divide jointly owned assets and liabilities as appropriate.

Identification block (top of page 2) 15 fields

Fifteen boxes that say who the statement belongs to, which business it supports, and the date every number is measured on.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
NameYour own name. The statement is personal: one person per form, with a second signature block for a spouse where one is required.Entering the business name here. The business has its own line below.
Business Phone (xxx-xxx-xxxx)A phone number for the business, in the format printed beside the box.Leaving it blank when a business number exists. Step 2 on page 1 is "Complete the form in its entirety."
Home AddressYour home street address.Entering the business address. That is a separate line.
Home PhoneYour home phone number.Leaving it blank without saying why.
City, State, & Zip CodeCity, state and ZIP code that go with the home address.Putting the city and ZIP on the Home Address line and leaving this one empty.
Business Name of Applicant/BorrowerThe name of the business that is applying, not your personal name.Using a different name than the one on the rest of the application package.
Business Address (if different than home address)The business address, only when it differs from your home address.Repeating the home address. The label says "if different."
Business Type: Corporation, S-Corp., LLC, Partnership, Sole Proprietor ×5Five checkboxes. Check the one that describes the applicant business.Leaving all five empty, or checking the type you plan to become rather than the type the business is on the statement date.
This information is current as of (mm/dd/yyyy)One date. Every balance and value on the form is the figure on that date. The form prints the rule under the box: the date "must be within 90 days of submission for Disaster or within 120 days of submission for 7(a)/504/SBG/8(a) BD/WOSB."A date that is outside the window by the time the form is submitted, or balances pulled from several different dates.
WOSB applicant only, Married: Yes / No ×2Two checkboxes, for WOSB applicants only. Check Yes or No.Checking both, or answering it when you are not a WOSB applicant.

Assets column 11 fields

Ten amount boxes and a total. The column heading says "(Omit Cents)", so enter whole dollars. Seven of the ten lines carry a "Describe in Section …" pointer; the amount here and the description there must tell the same story.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Cash on Hand & in banksCash you hold plus bank balances on the statement date, other than what you report on the Savings Accounts line.Counting the same account here and again under Savings Accounts.
Savings AccountsBalances in savings accounts on the statement date.Including cents, or rounding to a guess when a statement shows the balance.
IRA or Other Retirement AccountThe value of IRAs and other retirement accounts. The form says "(Describe in Section 5)."Entering an amount with no matching description in Section 5.
Accounts & Notes ReceivableMoney other people owe you personally. "(Describe in Section 5)."Listing amounts owed to the business. The form asks for personal information, not business information.
Life Insurance – Cash Surrender Value OnlyOnly the cash surrender value of your policies. "(Describe in Section 8)."Entering the face amount. The face amount belongs in Section 8, not in this box.
Stocks and BondsThe total value of your stocks and bonds. "(Describe in Section 3)."A figure that does not equal the Total Value column of Section 3.
Real EstateThe total present market value of real estate you own. "(Describe in Section 4)."Entering original cost, or equity after the mortgage. Section 4 has separate rows for cost, market value and mortgage balance; the mortgage goes in the liabilities column.
AutomobilesThe value of your vehicles. "(Describe in Section 5, and include Year/Make/Model)."Describing the vehicle in Section 5 without the year, make and model the form asks for.
Other Personal PropertyPersonal property not captured on the lines above. "(Describe in Section 5)."A lump sum with nothing in Section 5 to explain it.
Other AssetsAny asset that fits no other line. "(Describe in Section 5)."Using it for items that have their own line, such as retirement accounts or receivables.
Total (assets)The sum of the ten asset lines. In the official fillable PDF this box calculates itself.On a printed or flattened copy: an arithmetic slip, or a total left unchanged after a line above was edited.

Liabilities column 12 fields

Ten entry boxes, then Total Liabilities and Net Worth. Also whole dollars.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Accounts PayableBills you personally owe that are not notes, installment loans or mortgages.Entering business payables.
Notes Payable to Banks and OthersThe total you owe on notes. "(Describe in Section 2)."A figure that does not equal the Current Balance column of Section 2.
Installment Account (Auto)The balance still owed on auto installment loans.Entering the monthly payment here. It has its own box on the next line.
Mo. Payments (Auto)The monthly payment on those auto loans.A balance above with this box left blank.
Installment Account (Other)The balance owed on other installment accounts.Repeating a debt already counted under Notes Payable or Installment Account (Auto).
Mo. Payments (Other)The monthly payment on those other installment accounts.A balance above with this box left blank.
Loan(s) Against Life InsuranceWhat you have borrowed against life insurance policies.A policy loan here with no policy described in Section 8.
Mortgages on Real EstateThe total of your mortgage balances. "(Describe in Section 4)."A figure that does not equal the Mortgage Balance rows of Section 4 added together.
Unpaid TaxesTaxes you owe and have not paid. "(Describe in Section 6)."An amount here with Section 6 left empty. Section 6 asks for type, to whom payable, when due, amount, and any property a tax lien attaches to.
Other LiabilitiesDebts that fit no other line. "(Describe in Section 7)."An amount here with Section 7 left empty.
Total LiabilitiesThe sum of the liability balances. In the official fillable PDF this box calculates itself from the eight balance lines; the two Mo. Payments boxes are not part of the sum.On a printed copy: adding the monthly payments into the total.
Net WorthTotal assets minus Total Liabilities. The official fillable PDF calculates it for you.Typing a net worth from another document. On this form it is a result, not an estimate.

Section 1: Source of Income and Contingent Liabilities 9 fields

Four income boxes on the left, four contingent-liability boxes on the right, and one description box underneath.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
SalaryYour salary.Entering the business's revenue instead of your own salary.
Net Investment IncomeYour net income from investments.Entering gross investment income. The label says "Net."
Real Estate IncomeYour income from real estate.Reporting real estate income while Section 4 lists no property that could produce it.
Other Income (Describe below)Income that fits none of the three lines above.An amount here with the description box left empty.
As Endorser or Co-MakerAmounts you could become liable for because you endorsed or co-made someone else's debt.Skipping it because no payment is currently due from you. The heading is "Contingent Liabilities."
Legal Claims & JudgmentsAmounts of legal claims and judgments against you.Leaving it blank when a claim or judgment exists.
Provision for Federal Income TaxYour provision for federal income tax.Confusing it with Unpaid Taxes in the liabilities column, which is a separate line described in Section 6.
Other Special DebtAny other special debt that belongs under contingent liabilities.Repeating a debt already entered in the liabilities column.
Description of Other Income in Section 1A plain description of what the Other Income amount is. The form adds: "Alimony or child support payments should not be disclosed in 'Other Income' unless it is desired to have such payments counted toward total income."Leaving it blank when Other Income has an amount.

Section 2: Notes Payable to Banks and Others 30 fields

Five rows of six columns: 30 fields. One row per note. The form says: "Use attachments if necessary. Each attachment must be identified as part of this statement and signed."

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Names and Addresses of Noteholder(s) ×5Who you owe, with their address.A name with no address.
Original Balance ×5The amount of the note when it was made.Copying the current balance into this column.
Current Balance ×5What you still owe on the statement date.Rows that do not add up to Notes Payable to Banks and Others in the liabilities column.
Payment Amount ×5The amount of each payment.Leaving it blank on a note that has scheduled payments.
Frequency (monthly, etc.) ×5How often the payment is made.A payment amount with no frequency, so the reader cannot tell monthly from yearly.
How Secured or Endorsed / Type of Collateral ×5How the note is secured or endorsed and what the collateral is.Leaving it blank on a secured note.

Section 3: Stocks and Bonds 24 fields

Four rows of six columns: 24 fields. One row per security, with the same attachment rule as Section 2.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Number of Shares ×4How many shares you hold.Entering a dollar amount here.
Name of Securities ×4The name of the security.One row for a whole account with no security named.
Cost ×4What you paid.Entering today's value in the Cost column.
Market Value Quotation/Exchange ×4The quoted market value.Leaving it blank while Total Value is filled in.
Date of Quotation/Exchange ×4The date of that quotation.No date, so the quotation cannot be compared with the statement date.
Total Value ×4The total value of the holding.Rows that do not add up to Stocks and Bonds in the assets column.

Section 4: Real Estate Owned 30 fields

Three columns (Property A, B, C) of ten rows: 30 fields. The form says "List each parcel separately" and repeats the attachment rule.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Type of Real Estate ×3The kind of property. The form gives examples: "Primary Residence, Other Residence, Rental Property, Land, etc."Leaving the type off, or combining two parcels in one column.
Address ×3The address of the property.An incomplete address.
Date Purchased ×3The date you bought it.Leaving it blank.
Original Cost ×3What you paid for it.Entering today's value. That is the next row.
Present Market Value ×3What the property is worth on the statement date.Values that do not add up to Real Estate in the assets column.
Name & Address of Mortgage Holder ×3Who holds the mortgage, with their address.A mortgage balance below with no holder named.
Mortgage Account Number ×3The account number of the mortgage.Leaving it blank on a mortgaged property.
Mortgage Balance ×3What you still owe on the statement date.Balances that do not add up to Mortgages on Real Estate in the liabilities column.
Amount of Payment per Month/Year ×3The payment amount, and whether it is per month or per year.A number with no indication of month or year.
Status of Mortgage ×3The status of the mortgage.Leaving it blank.

Sections 5 to 8: description boxes 4 fields

Four free-text boxes. Each one explains amounts you entered in the two columns on page 2.

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Section 5. Other Personal Property and Other AssetsDescribe the retirement accounts, receivables, automobiles (with year, make and model), other personal property and other assets from the assets column. The form adds: "if any is pledged as security, state name and address of lien holder, amount of lien, terms of payment and, if delinquent, describe delinquency."Describing only some of the five asset lines that point here.
Section 6. Unpaid TaxesThe form asks you to "Describe in detail as to type, to whom payable, when due, amount, and to what property, if any, a tax lien attaches."Giving an amount only.
Section 7. Other Liabilities"Describe in detail" the Other Liabilities amount.Leaving it blank when Other Liabilities has an amount.
Section 8. Life Insurance HeldThe form asks for "face amount and cash surrender value of policies – name of insurance company and Beneficiaries."Giving the face amount but not the cash surrender value, which is the figure the assets column uses.

Authorization, certification and signatures (page 4) 8 fields

Two identical signature blocks of four fields each. The certification says it is completed "by each person submitting the information requested on this form and the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included."

FieldWhat to enterCommon mistake
Signature ×2Your signature in the first block; a spouse's in the second where one is required. By signing you certify "under penalty of criminal prosecution" that the information is true and complete to the best of your knowledge.Submitting unsigned, or without the spouse's signature when spousal assets are included.
Date (mm/dd/yyyy) ×2The date each person signs.Leaving it blank, or confusing it with the "current as of" date on page 2. They are separate boxes.
Print Name ×2The printed name of each signer.A signature with no printed name beside it.
Social Security No. (xxx-xx-xxxx) ×2Each signer's Social Security number, in the printed format.Leaving it blank. The Privacy Act notice on page 6 explains when the number is required and when it is voluntary.

Boxes the official PDF fills in for you

Three of the 147 fields calculate themselves in the official fillable PDF: Total (assets), Total Liabilities and Net Worth. The form also prints a second "Total" at the bottom of the liabilities column with the note "Must equal total in assets column." In the PDF that box repeats the assets total, which is why the form has 147 fields but 148 boxes.

The calculations stop there. The PDF does not check that the amounts on page 2 agree with the detail in Sections 2, 3 and 4, and it does not check the date window. Those are the checks to make yourself before you sign:

  • Notes Payable to Banks and Others equals the Current Balance column of Section 2.
  • Stocks and Bonds equals the Total Value column of Section 3.
  • Real Estate equals the Present Market Value rows of Section 4, and Mortgages on Real Estate equals the Mortgage Balance rows.
  • Every line marked "Describe in Section …" has a description in that section.
  • The "current as of" date is within 90 days of submission for Disaster, or within 120 days for 7(a), 504, surety bond, 8(a) BD and WOSB.
  • Each required person has signed, dated, printed their name and entered a Social Security number.

Pages 5 and 6 have nothing to fill in

Page 5 prints the notices on criminal penalties and administrative remedies for false statements, one for each program, and the Paperwork Reduction Act note. Page 6 prints the Privacy Act, Right to Financial Privacy Act and Freedom of Information Act notices under the heading "PLEASE READ, DETACH, AND RETAIN FOR YOUR RECORDS." The certification you sign on page 4 says you have read these statements, so read them before you sign.
