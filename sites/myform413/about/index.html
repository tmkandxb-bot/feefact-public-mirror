About MyForm413

MyForm413 does one thing: it helps you fill out SBA Form 413, the Personal Financial Statement, and gives you the official PDF with your answers in it, ready to sign and date.

Why this site exists

Form 413 is six pages long, and the official PDF has 147 fillable fields. The form does not add anything up for you. Under the liabilities column it prints "Must equal total in assets column," and most lines on page 2 point to a detail section on pages 3 and 4 that has to be filled in as well.

The MyForm413 wizard asks for the same information one step at a time. It does the arithmetic, checks that the two columns balance, and cross-checks the detail sections against your summary totals while you type. Then it fills the official PDF.

The guides on this site use only what the official form and its instructions say. Where the form does not answer a question, we leave it out.

Not affiliated with the SBA

MyForm413 is a private website. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or approved by the U.S. Small Business Administration or any lender.
  • We do not receive, review or submit your form. You send it yourself to whoever asked for it: the form says to return it to the lender for 7(a) loans, the Certified Development Company (CDC) for 504 loans, and the surety company or agent for surety bonds.
  • We do not decide anything about your application, and using this tool does not guarantee any outcome.
  • Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice. You are responsible for checking that what you submit is accurate and complete.
  • The SBA publishes Form 413 itself, free of charge: SBA Form 413 document page and the official PDF.

How we keep the form current

  • We fill the SBA's own PDF. The tool writes your answers into the official form. It does not produce a look-alike.
  • We compare our copy with the SBA's. The edition and the date of the last check are shown in the table above.
  • When the SBA publishes a new edition, we update the tool, the guides and this page, and we record the change here.
  • We name the edition on our pages, so you always know which form a page is describing.

Before you submit, compare the footer of your PDF with the form on the SBA's site. If the two do not match, use the SBA's form.
