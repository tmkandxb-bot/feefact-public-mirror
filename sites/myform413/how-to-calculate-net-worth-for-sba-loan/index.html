Net Worth Calculator for SBA Loans: How to Calculate Net Worth on Form 413

A net worth calculator adds up what you own and subtracts what you owe. For an SBA loan, that number is a line on SBA Form 413, the Personal Financial Statement, built from the assets and liabilities columns on page 2 of the form, as of one date. This post shows how to calculate net worth for an SBA loan the way the current form (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188) lays it out, so you know what the net worth calculator is doing.

The formula

Net worth = total assets − total liabilities

At the bottom of the liabilities column, Total Liabilities and Net Worth are added together, and the form says that total "must equal total in assets column." This checks your arithmetic only. If the two column totals differ, redo the sums. A statement that balances can still be missing an asset or a debt, so go through the lines yourself to confirm nothing was left out.

Step 1: Pick one date

The form asks for the date the information is "current as of," and every balance should be for that date. The form limits how old it can be: within 120 days of submission for 7(a), 504, surety bond, 8(a) BD and WOSB applications, and within 90 days of submission for Disaster loans.

Step 2: Add up your assets

The assets column on page 2 has ten lines, in whole dollars (the column is headed "Omit Cents"):

  1. Cash on hand and in banks
  2. Savings accounts
  3. IRA or other retirement account
  4. Accounts and notes receivable
  5. Life insurance, cash surrender value only
  6. Stocks and bonds
  7. Real estate
  8. Automobiles
  9. Other personal property
  10. Other assets

Two lines are easy to overstate. Life insurance counts at its cash surrender value only, not the face amount. Real estate goes in at its value, with the mortgage entered separately as a liability: Section 4 of the form asks for each property's present market value and its mortgage balance side by side.

Step 3: Add up your liabilities

The liabilities column on page 2 has eight lines:

  1. Accounts payable
  2. Notes payable to banks and others
  3. Installment account (auto)
  4. Installment account (other)
  5. Loan(s) against life insurance
  6. Mortgages on real estate
  7. Unpaid taxes
  8. Other liabilities

Enter the balance still owed. The two installment lines also have a box for the monthly payment, but the payment is not part of the total.

Step 4: Subtract, then check the arithmetic

Total assets minus total liabilities is your net worth. Write it on the Net Worth line, add it to Total Liabilities, and confirm the result equals the assets total.

A worked example

These numbers are made up to show the arithmetic. They are not a benchmark or a target.

AssetsAmountLiabilitiesAmount
Cash on hand & in banks$12,000Accounts payable$3,000
Savings accounts$20,000Installment account (auto)$10,000
IRA or other retirement account$85,000Mortgages on real estate$260,000
Real estate$400,000Unpaid taxes$2,000
Automobiles$18,000Total liabilities$275,000
Other personal property$5,000Net worth$265,000
Total$540,000Total$540,000

$540,000 − $275,000 = $265,000. Total liabilities plus net worth ($275,000 + $265,000) equals the $540,000 assets total, so the arithmetic checks out.

What is not in the number

  • Income. Salary, net investment income, real estate income and other income are listed in Section 1, separately from the two columns.
  • Contingent liabilities. Amounts as endorser or co-maker, legal claims and judgments, provision for federal income tax and other special debt have their own block in Section 1, outside the liabilities column.
  • Business figures. The form says to complete it "with Personal Information not Business Information."

Whose assets and debts count

The form says to "divide all jointly owned assets and liabilities, as appropriate with spouse or others."

Who completes a form. For 7(a) and 504 loans, page 1 says to complete the form for each proprietor, each general partner, each managing member of an LLC, each owner of 20% or more of the applicant's equity ("including the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children"), and any person providing a guaranty on the loan. Each of them has a statement, and a net worth figure, of their own.

Who signs. The certification on page 4 is completed by each person submitting the information on the form, and by "the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included."

A different "personal net worth" for 8(a) and EDWOSB

The same form is used by people who must show economic disadvantage: individuals qualifying a firm for the 8(a) Business Development program, and women claiming economic disadvantage in the WOSB program (the EDWOSB designation). It does not apply to every WOSB applicant. For those two cases the form describes a separate test, one part of which is less than $850,000 in personal net worth, excluding equity in the personal residence, ownership interest in the business, and funds in a retirement account that are unavailable until retirement age. That adjusted figure is not the Net Worth line described above, and the form states no such threshold for 7(a), 504 or Disaster loans.
