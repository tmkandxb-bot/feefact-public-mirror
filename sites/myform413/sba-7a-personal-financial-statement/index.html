SBA 7(a) Personal Financial Statement: Who Files Form 413 and Where It Goes

The personal financial statement for an SBA 7(a) loan is SBA Form 413. This page covers only what is specific to a 7(a) application: who has to complete one, which box to check, how recent the numbers must be, who signs, and who receives it. Everything here follows the current form (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188, expires 08/31/2027).

Which form a 7(a) loan uses

There is no separate 7(a) statement. SBA Form 413, titled "Personal Financial Statement," is one form shared by several programs: 7(a) loans, 504 loans, surety bonds, disaster business loans, the Women-Owned Small Business program and the 8(a) Business Development program. The first page lists the programs with a checkbox beside each, and the form's own instruction is to "check all that apply." For a 7(a) loan, check the box labeled 7(a) loan / 504 loan / Surety Bonds.

The form states what SBA does with it: the information is "one of a number of data sources in analyzing the repayment ability and creditworthiness of an applicant for an SBA loan."

Who has to complete one for a 7(a) loan

Under the 7(a) / 504 / Surety Bonds heading, the form says to complete it for:

  1. each proprietor;
  2. each general partner;
  3. each managing member of a limited liability company (LLC);
  4. each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant business; and
  5. any person providing a guaranty on the loan.

For an owner of 20% or more, the form adds that the statement includes "the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children." Each person on the list completes a statement of their own. If you are not sure whether you fall into one of these groups, ask the lender handling the application.

How recent the numbers must be

The form asks for the date the information is current as of, and prints the limit beside it: for 7(a) the date must be within 120 days of submission. Pick one date and use balances and values as of that date throughout the form.

Who signs

The certification at the end is completed by each person submitting the information, and by the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included. That is why the form has two signature blocks, each with a signature, date, printed name and Social Security number.

Signing does three things the form spells out:

  • You certify, under penalty of criminal prosecution, that the form and any supporting information submitted with it are true and complete to the best of your knowledge.
  • You acknowledge that SBA or its participating lenders will rely on the information when deciding on the application.
  • You authorize the SBA and the lender to make inquiries as necessary to verify the statements and determine your creditworthiness.

The form's notice to 7(a) applicants says that knowingly making a false statement is a violation of federal law and could result in criminal prosecution, significant civil penalties, and denial of the loan application.

Where the completed form goes

For a 7(a) loan, the form says to return it to the lender processing the application for SBA guaranty. Your lender tells you how it wants the form delivered.

Quick check before you hand it to your lender

  1. The 7(a) loan / 504 loan / Surety Bonds box is checked.
  2. Everyone on the list above has a completed statement.
  3. The "current as of" date is within 120 days of the day you submit.
  4. Total assets equals total liabilities plus net worth. The form prints "Must equal total in assets column" under the liabilities side.
  5. Amounts are in whole dollars. The form's columns are marked "Omit Cents."
  6. Any attachment is identified as part of the statement and signed, as Sections 2 through 4 require.
  7. The form is signed and dated, including by a spouse where spousal assets are included.
