SBA Form 413 Example: A Filled-Out Personal Financial Statement

A blank SBA Form 413 is easier to complete once you have seen a finished one. This page shows a personal financial statement example filled in from the header to the signature, following the current form (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188, expires 08/31/2027) section by section, with sample numbers that balance.

The line names, section names and quoted instructions are taken from the official form. Nothing here is legal, tax, or accounting advice, and a completed form does not predict any decision on an application.

The example in one paragraph

Alex Morgan owns Morgan Bakery LLC and is applying for an SBA 7(a) loan. Alex has a checking and a savings account, a retirement account, two investment holdings, a home with a mortgage, one financed car, household belongings, a personal bank loan, a small student loan, a small accounts-payable balance, a life insurance policy with no cash value, and a relative’s loan that Alex co-signed. Those facts are enough to touch every section of the form, including the ones that end up saying “None.”

Under the 7(a) / 504 / Surety Bonds box, the form says who completes it: each proprietor, general partner, managing member of an LLC, each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant “(including the assets of the owner’s spouse and any minor children),” and any person providing a guaranty on the loan. In this example Alex has no spouse and no minor children, so every figure on the statement is Alex’s own. For a 7(a) loan, the completed form goes back to the lender processing the application.

Net worth is not a number you choose. It is what is left when total liabilities are taken away from total assets: $650,000 − $293,000 = $357,000. The last line of the liabilities column then adds the two back together, which is why the form says it must equal the assets total.

Certification and signature

Above the signature lines, the form has you authorize the SBA, lender or surety company to make inquiries to verify the statement, and then certify, under penalty of criminal prosecution, that all information on the form is true and complete to the best of your knowledge. Each signature block has four parts:

FieldExample entry
SignatureAlex Morgan (signed)
Date (mm/dd/yyyy)The day Alex actually signs
Print NameAlex Morgan

There is a second, identical block. The certification is to be completed by each person submitting the information and by the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included. In this example no spousal assets are included, so only Alex signs and the second block is left blank.

Using this as a model for your own statement

  1. Pick your “as of” date first and take every balance on that date. Check it against the form’s window: 90 days before submission for Disaster, 120 days for 7(a)/504/SBG/8(a) BD/WOSB.
  2. Fill in the detail sections that apply to you — Sections 2 through 8 — and carry their totals into Section 1.
  3. Add each column and subtract total liabilities from total assets to get net worth.
  4. Run the links in the table above on your own numbers.
  5. Sign and date after the numbers are final.

If a section needs more room than the form gives, Sections 2, 3 and 4 say to use attachments, and each attachment must be identified as part of the statement and signed.

Questions about this example

Is this SBA Form 413 example a real person’s statement?

No. The name, business, addresses and every dollar amount are made up to show how a completed form fits together. Only the form’s own lines, section names and instructions come from the official SBA Form 413 (version 05-24).

Can I copy the numbers from this personal financial statement example?

No. Use the layout as a model and enter your own figures. By signing Form 413 you certify, under penalty of criminal prosecution, that the information on it is true and complete to the best of your knowledge.

How do I know a filled-out Form 413 balances?

On the form, the liabilities column ends with total liabilities plus net worth, and that total must equal the total in the assets column. In this example, $293,000 in liabilities plus $357,000 of net worth equals $650,000 in assets.
