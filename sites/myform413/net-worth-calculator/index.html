How to calculate your net worth

Net worth is one subtraction. Add up the value of everything you own, add up everything you owe, and take the second number away from the first:

Net worth = total assets − total liabilities

The result is a snapshot as of one date. It changes whenever a balance changes, so pick a date — usually today — and use the figures for that date on every line.

  1. List your assets at today's value. Use the current balance for accounts and a realistic present market value for property — what it would sell for now, not what you paid.
  2. List your liabilities at today's balance. Use the amount still owed on each debt. Monthly payments do not go into a net worth calculation.
  3. Subtract. Total assets minus total liabilities is your net worth. The calculator above does both totals and the subtraction for you.

$475,000 − $275,000 = $200,000 net worth. Notice that the home appears twice: its full market value on the asset side and the mortgage on the liability side. The $110,000 difference between them is the home equity, and it is already inside the $200,000.

What counts as an asset

An asset is anything you own that has a money value today. The calculator uses ten lines:

  • Cash on hand and in banks and savings accounts — current balances.
  • IRA or other retirement account — the current account balance.
  • Accounts and notes receivable — money owed to you personally.
  • Life insurance — the cash surrender value only. A policy's death benefit is not an asset you hold today.
  • Stocks and bonds — current market value.
  • Real estate — present market value of each property, with the mortgage entered separately as a liability.
  • Automobiles — what the vehicle would sell for now.
  • Other personal property and other assets — anything else of value.

What counts as a liability

A liability is any amount you owe. The calculator uses eight lines:

  • Accounts payable — bills you owe now, such as credit card balances.
  • Notes payable to banks and others — personal loans, lines of credit, student loans.
  • Installment accounts (auto and other) — the remaining balance.
  • Loans against life insurance.
  • Mortgages on real estate — the remaining balance on every property loan.
  • Unpaid taxes and other liabilities.

Common mistakes that distort the result

  • Entering a home's equity instead of its value. If you enter equity as the asset and also enter the mortgage, the mortgage is subtracted twice.
  • Using purchase price. Net worth uses what something is worth today.
  • Counting a life insurance death benefit. Only the cash surrender value belongs here.
  • Entering monthly payments as debts. Enter the full balance still owed.
  • Mixing dates. A bank balance from last month and a loan balance from today describe two different moments.
  • Leaving out small debts. Credit card balances and unpaid taxes are liabilities too.

What if the number is negative?

A negative net worth means your liabilities are larger than your assets. The calculator shows it with a minus sign and still works normally. Net worth is a measurement at one point in time; it says nothing about income or about what the number will be next year.

Net worth on SBA Form 413

If you are applying for an SBA loan, your net worth is not just a number for yourself — it is a line on SBA Form 413, the Personal Financial Statement. That is why this calculator uses exactly the asset and liability lines in Section 1 of the current form (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188, expires 08/31/2027). What the form itself says:

  • For 7(a) loans, 504 loans and surety bonds, the form is completed for each proprietor, general partner, managing member of an LLC, each owner of 20% or more of the applicant's equity, and any person providing a guaranty on the loan.
  • Amounts are entered in whole dollars ("Omit Cents").
  • The form asks for personal information, not business information, and tells you to divide jointly owned assets and liabilities, as appropriate, with your spouse or others.
  • The statement date must be within 90 days of submission for Disaster loans, or within 120 days for 7(a), 504, surety bond, 8(a) BD and WOSB.
  • Several Section 1 lines are backed by detail schedules later in the form — notes payable (Section 2), stocks and bonds (Section 3), real estate (Section 4) — and the summary should agree with them.

One caution: for the 8(a) Business Development and WOSB programs, the form describes a separate "personal net worth" test that leaves out certain items, such as equity in a personal residence. This calculator does not compute that adjusted figure; it computes the plain assets-minus-liabilities net worth shown in Section 1.

Source: SBA Form 413 (05-24), published by the U.S. Small Business Administration: official PDF and document page on legacy.sba.gov.

Net worth calculator FAQ

How do you calculate net worth?

Add up the current value of everything you own (your assets), add up everything you owe (your liabilities), and subtract the second total from the first. Net worth = total assets − total liabilities. The calculator on this page does the addition and subtraction as you type.

What counts as an asset?

Anything you own that has a money value today: cash and bank balances, retirement accounts, investments, real estate at its present market value, vehicles, the cash surrender value of life insurance, money owed to you, and other personal property.

What counts as a liability?

Any amount you owe: credit card and other unpaid bills, personal and student loans, car loans, mortgages and home equity loans, loans against life insurance, and unpaid taxes. Use the remaining balance, not the monthly payment.

Can net worth be negative?

Yes. If you owe more than you own, the result is a negative number. The calculator shows it with a minus sign. It is arithmetic, not a judgment.

Is the equity in my home part of my net worth?

Yes. Enter the home's present market value under Real estate and the remaining mortgage balance under Mortgages on real estate. The difference between the two is your home equity, and it flows into the result automatically.

Is this the same net worth that goes on SBA Form 413?

The lines match Section 1 of the current SBA Form 413 (version 05-24): ten asset lines, eight liability lines, and net worth as the difference. The form also asks you to omit cents and to divide jointly owned assets and liabilities with your spouse or others as appropriate.
