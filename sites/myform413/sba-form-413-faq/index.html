SBA Form 413 FAQ

Short answers to the questions people ask most about the SBA Personal Financial Statement. Every answer comes from the official SBA Form 413 (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188), so you can check it against the form itself. Where the form does not say, this page does not guess.

The basics

What is SBA Form 413?

SBA Form 413 is the U.S. Small Business Administration's Personal Financial Statement. The form says its purpose is "to collect information about the Business Applicant and its owners' financial condition." It lists your personal assets, liabilities, income and contingent liabilities as of one date. For a walk-through, see how to fill out SBA Form 413, line by line.

Which SBA programs use Form 413?

The top of the form has four boxes, and you check all that apply: 7(a) loan / 504 loan / Surety Bonds, Disaster Business Loan Application, Women Owned Small Business (WOSB) Federal Contracting Program, and 8(a) Business Development Program.

What does the SBA use the form for?

According to the form, the SBA uses it as one of a number of data sources in analyzing the repayment ability and creditworthiness of a loan applicant. For a surety bond it is used to assist in recovery if the contractor defaults on the contract. For the WOSB and 8(a) BD programs it is used to assess whether an individual meets the economic disadvantage threshold.

What is the current version of Form 413?

The current edition is dated 05-24. Every page footer reads "SBA Form 413 (05-24) Previous Editions Obsolete," and the top of page 1 shows OMB Approval No. 3245-0188 with an expiration date of 08/31/2027.

Where can I get the official form?

The SBA publishes it on the SBA Form 413 document page, with a direct PDF download. Every answer on this page is taken from that PDF.

How long does Form 413 take to complete?

The Paperwork Reduction Act notice on page 5 of the form gives an estimated average burden of 1.5 hours per response.

Who files it, when, and where it goes

Who has to complete Form 413?

It depends on the program box you check. For 7(a), 504 and surety bonds the form lists each proprietor, general partner, managing member of an LLC, each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant, and any person providing a guaranty on the loan. Disaster, WOSB and 8(a) each have their own list. Full answer: Who must complete Form 413?

How recent does the statement have to be?

Next to the "This information is current as of" date, the form says the date must be within 90 days of submission for Disaster, or within 120 days of submission for 7(a), 504, SBG, 8(a) BD and WOSB. Full answer: How recent must Form 413 be?

Where do I send the completed form?

The form names a recipient for each program. For 7(a) loans it is the lender processing the application; for 504 loans, the Certified Development Company (CDC); for surety bonds, the surety company or agent. Disaster business loan applicants return it to the Disaster Processing and Disbursement Center. For WOSB and 8(a), the form says the information must be submitted and certified through the SBA certification website it names.

Do I have to update the form later?

For the WOSB program, the form says each individual claiming economic disadvantage must update it "as changes arise, but at least annually." The form does not print an update rule for the other programs; ask whoever is processing your application.

Spouses

Does my spouse have to sign Form 413?

The certification on page 4 is completed by each person submitting the form "and the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included." Under WOSB and 8(a), a spouse completes a separate form instead, unless the couple is legally separated. Full answer: Does a spouse have to sign Form 413?

Do I include my spouse's assets?

For 7(a), 504 and surety bonds, the form says the statement of an owner of 20% or more of the applicant includes "the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children." The form also carries a note to divide jointly owned assets and liabilities, as appropriate, with a spouse or others; that note is printed in the 8(a) instructions.

Filling in the numbers

Do I enter personal or business figures?

Personal. A note in the 8(a) instructions on page 1 says to complete the form "with Personal Information not Business Information." The business name, address and type go in the header on page 2.

Do the two columns have to balance?

Yes. The liabilities column ends with Total Liabilities, then Net Worth, then a Total, and under it the form prints "Must equal total in assets column." Net worth is total assets minus total liabilities, so the two totals match when the arithmetic is right.

Do I enter cents?

No. Both the assets column and the liabilities column are headed "(Omit Cents)."

Which lines need more detail further down the form?

Many lines on page 2 point to a section on pages 3 and 4. Notes Payable goes to Section 2, Stocks and Bonds to Section 3, Real Estate and Mortgages on Real Estate to Section 4, retirement accounts, receivables, automobiles, other personal property and other assets to Section 5, Unpaid Taxes to Section 6, Other Liabilities to Section 7, and Life Insurance to Section 8. The field-by-field instructions cover each one.

Do I have to list alimony or child support as income?

Not unless you want it counted. The form says alimony or child support payments "should not be disclosed in 'Other Income' unless it is desired to have such payments counted toward total income."

What goes under contingent liabilities?

Section 1 has four contingent liability lines: As Endorser or Co-Maker, Legal Claims & Judgments, Provision for Federal Income Tax, and Other Special Debt.

What if I run out of room?

Use attachments. Sections 2, 3 and 4 each say attachments may be used and that "each attachment must be identified as part of this statement and signed."

What are the most common things to check before submitting?

The date window, the balance line, the detail sections, attachments and signatures. The pre-submission checklist goes through them one by one, and the filled-out example shows what a complete form looks like.

Signing and certification

What am I certifying when I sign?

By signing, you certify "under penalty of criminal prosecution that all information on this form and any additional supporting information submitted with this form is true and complete to the best of my knowledge." You also authorize the SBA, lender or surety company to make inquiries to verify the statements and determine your creditworthiness.

What does the signature block ask for?

A signature, date, printed name and Social Security number. Page 4 has two sets of these lines.

Do I have to give my Social Security number?

The Privacy Act notice on page 6 says loan applicants and guarantors, and any indemnitor of a surety bond, are required to provide a Social Security number or other taxpayer identification number under 31 U.S.C. 7701. For other individuals signing the form, it says submission of the number is voluntary.

What happens if a statement on the form is false?

Page 5 carries a separate notice for each program. For 7(a), 504 and surety bond applicants it says that knowingly making a false statement is a violation of Federal law and could result in criminal prosecution, significant civil penalties, and a denial of the loan or surety bond application. Read the notice for your program before you sign.
