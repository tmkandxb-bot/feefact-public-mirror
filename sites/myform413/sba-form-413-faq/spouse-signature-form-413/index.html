Does My Spouse Have to Sign SBA Form 413?

Short answer: your spouse signs your Form 413 when you own 20% or more of the applicant business and the statement includes your spouse's assets. Under the WOSB and 8(a) programs the rule is different: your spouse completes a separate Form 413 of their own, unless you are legally separated.

Everything below is taken from the official SBA Form 413 (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188). The form answers this question in three places.

When your spouse signs your form

The certification on page 4 is "to be completed by each person submitting the information requested on this form and the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included."

For 7(a) loans, 504 loans and surety bonds, page 1 says the form is completed for each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant, "including the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children." So a 20% or more owner under these programs reports a spouse's assets on the statement, and the spouse signs the same form.

Page 4 has two signature blocks under the certification. Each one has its own signature, date, printed name and Social Security number line.

When your spouse fills out a separate form

  • WOSB Federal Contracting Program. "A separate form must be completed by the individual's spouse, unless the individual and the spouse are legally separated." Page 2 also has a "WOSB applicant only, Married Yes / No" box.
  • 8(a) Business Development Program. "If married, the spouse must complete a separate SBA Form 413, except when the individual and the spouse are legally separated. If separated, provide copy of separation document." The 8(a) note also says to divide all jointly owned assets and liabilities, as appropriate, with the spouse or others.

Disaster business loans

The Disaster Business Loan paragraph on page 1 lists who completes the form and does not mention a spouse. The certification wording on page 4 is the same for every program, so it still applies if spousal assets are included on a 20% or more owner's statement.

What the form does not say

The form gives no rule based on the state you live in, and none for a spouse who has no ownership and whose assets are not on the statement. The first page only says to sign with "spousal signature, if required." If your case is not one of those above, ask the lender, Certified Development Company, surety company or SBA office receiving your form before you submit it.

A spouse who signs makes the same certification you do: under penalty of criminal prosecution, that the information is true and complete to the best of their knowledge. Give them the finished statement to read before they sign.
