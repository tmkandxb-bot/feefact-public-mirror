Personal Financial Statement for an SBA Loan: A Borrower's Guide

When a lender asks for a personal financial statement for an SBA loan, it means one specific document: SBA Form 413. This guide covers what a borrower needs to know before filling it in — who has to complete one, who it goes to, how recent it must be, and what you are signing. Everything here is taken from the current official form (version 05-24, OMB No. 3245-0188, expires 08/31/2027).

What the SBA loan personal financial statement is

Form 413 collects information about the business applicant's owners and their personal financial condition. The form states its purpose directly: SBA uses the information "as one of a number of data sources in analyzing the repayment ability and creditworthiness of an applicant for an SBA loan." It also says that submitting it is required as part of your application, and that failing to provide the information would impact the agency's decision.

It is a personal document. You report what you own, what you owe, your net worth, your sources of income and your contingent liabilities, as of one date, and back up the summary with detail sections.

Who has to complete one

The first page of the form lists who must complete it for each program. For a loan, the two lists are:

7(a) loan / 504 loan

  1. Each proprietor
  2. General partner
  3. Managing member of a limited liability company (LLC)
  4. Each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant (including the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children)
  5. Any person providing a guaranty on the loan

Disaster business loan

  1. Each applicant
  2. Each general partner
  3. Each managing member of a limited liability company (LLC)
  4. Each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant business
  5. Any person providing an unlimited guaranty on the loan

So a business with three owners at 40%, 40% and 20% needs three statements, one per person. If someone outside the ownership group guarantees the loan, that person completes one too.

The same form is also used for surety bond guarantees and for the WOSB and 8(a) programs, which have their own instructions on page 1. This guide covers loans only.

Who you give it to

You do not send a 7(a) or 504 statement to SBA yourself. The form says to return it to:

  • 7(a) loans: the lender processing the application for SBA guaranty.
  • 504 loans: the Certified Development Company (CDC) processing the application for SBA guaranty.
  • Disaster business loans: SBA's Disaster Processing and Disbursement Center, at the address, fax number or email printed on page 1 of the form.

How recent it has to be

The form asks for a "current as of" date and prints the limit next to it:

Application typeInformation must be current within
7(a), 504, surety bond, 8(a) BD, WOSB120 days of submission
Disaster90 days of submission

If your application takes longer than that to submit, expect to prepare a fresh statement with updated balances and a new date.

What the form asks for

  • Identification: name, home address and phones, the business name and address, the business type (Corporation, S-Corp., LLC, Partnership or Sole Proprietor), and the "current as of" date.
  • Assets: cash on hand and in banks, savings accounts, IRA or other retirement accounts, accounts and notes receivable, life insurance (cash surrender value only), stocks and bonds, real estate, automobiles, other personal property, and other assets.
  • Liabilities: accounts payable, notes payable to banks and others, installment accounts for autos and for other debts (each with the monthly payment), loans against life insurance, mortgages on real estate, unpaid taxes, and other liabilities.
  • Net worth: total liabilities plus net worth "must equal total in assets column."
  • Source of income: salary, net investment income, real estate income, and other income.
  • Contingent liabilities: as endorser or co-maker, legal claims and judgments, provision for federal income tax, and other special debt.
  • Detail sections 2 to 8: notes payable, stocks and bonds, real estate owned, other personal property and other assets, unpaid taxes, other liabilities, and life insurance held.

For how to fill in each line, see the line-by-line Form 413 guide. For how a lender reads the finished statement, see the personal financial statement, explained for borrowers.

Rules printed on the form that borrowers miss

  • Whole dollars. The asset and liability columns are headed "Omit Cents."
  • Complete all of it. The instructions say to complete the form in its entirety and attach a separate sheet if necessary.
  • Attachments are signed. Sections 2, 3 and 4 say each attachment must be identified as part of the statement and signed.
  • Alimony and child support are optional income. The form says these payments should not be disclosed in "Other Income" unless you want them counted toward total income.
  • Automobiles need detail. The automobiles line says to describe them in Section 5 and include year, make and model.
  • Life insurance is cash surrender value only on the asset line; Section 8 asks for both the face amount and the cash surrender value.

What you sign

Above the signature the form carries an authorization and a certification:

  • You authorize SBA or the lender to make inquiries as necessary to verify the accuracy of the statements and to determine your creditworthiness.
  • You certify, under penalty of criminal prosecution, that all information on the form and any supporting information submitted with it is true and complete to the best of your knowledge.
  • The certification is completed by each person submitting the information, and by the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included. The form has two signature blocks, each with a date, printed name and Social Security number.

The notices attached to the form state that knowingly making a false statement is a violation of federal law and could result in criminal prosecution, significant civil penalties, and denial of the loan application.

How long it takes

The form's Paperwork Reduction Act notice estimates an average of 1.5 hours per response. Most of that time goes into finding balances, not writing them down, so collect your bank, retirement, brokerage, loan, mortgage and life insurance statements first.

Before you hand it over

  1. Everyone on the form's list for your loan type has a statement of their own.
  2. The "current as of" date is inside the 120-day window (90 days for Disaster).
  3. Total liabilities plus net worth equals total assets.
  4. Every line that says "Describe in Section…" has a matching entry in that section.
  5. The form is signed and dated, with the spouse's signature where spousal assets are included.
  6. It is going to the right place: your lender for 7(a), your CDC for 504.

A complete statement does not guarantee any decision on a loan application. It removes the avoidable reasons for a lender to send the form back.
